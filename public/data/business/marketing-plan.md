## 1. Brand Identity & Positioning

### Brand Statement / Elevator Pitch

> "ANIMAL is the rare DJ/producer who doesn't just play music — he understands it from the inside out. Twenty-five years behind the decks, a classical foundation from age six, engineering chops forged under legends who shaped Norah Jones and AC/DC, and a Crooklyn Clan catalog that stretches from Eminem to Lorde. He doesn't just read the room — he's been studying rooms since before most of his competition could drive."

### Short Version (10 seconds)

> "Twenty-five years, a piano at age six, studios with the greats, and a career that's run from the Playboy Mansion to your favorite festival. ANIMAL is what happens when a musician becomes a DJ — not the other way around."

### Unique Selling Proposition

Most DJs are DJs. ANIMAL is a musician who became a DJ, and that distinction is everything.

#### The USP Stack — Each Layer Compounds the Others

| Layer | What It Is | Why It Matters |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| Classical foundation | Piano since age 6, percussion, vocals | Harmonic instincts that most producers never develop |
| Engineering pedigree | SAE NYC / trained under Danny Wyatt, John Siket, Mike White | He hears records differently — production decisions are informed, not guessed |
| Crooklyn Clan co-sign | Remix credits across hip-hop's elite | Credibility bridge between EDM and mainstream/urban — rare currency |
| Residency legacy | Playboy Mansion, WMC, Groove Cruise | Proven draw in aspirational, high-profile environments |
| Radio pioneer | First EDM FM mixshow in Raleigh (Curtis Media) | Media access and legitimacy most DJs can't claim |
| Genre fluidity | Electronic, but with hip-hop, pop, and live instrument DNA | Books across scenes — not locked into one bubble |

**The one-liner USP:**

> ANIMAL has the technical training of a session musician, the catalog of a top-tier remixer, and the stage presence of a headliner. He plays sets — he doesn't just press play.

### Tone of Voice Guide

#### Social Media (Instagram, TikTok, X)

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Short punchy captions. Humor when it's real. Name-drops only when they add context, not clout. | Hyperbolic hype ("INSANE DROP 🔥🔥🔥"). Overly curated/filtered. Posting without substance. |

**Example tone:**
> "25 years in and the studio still hits different at 2am. New one coming."

*(vs. "SO EXCITED TO SHARE MY INCREDIBLE NEW TRACK WITH YOU ALL!!!")*

#### Press & PR

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Lean into the narrative arc (classical → production training → DJ → pioneer). Make it a story, not a resume. | Generic bio copy. Passive voice. Burying the Crooklyn Clan connection. |

**Example angle:**
> "The first FM EDM broadcast in Raleigh wasn't a fluke — it was the natural next step for a musician who'd spent two decades learning every layer of how sound works."

#### Email (Fans)

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Short subject lines. Real news. Actual value (early links, presale codes, behind-the-scenes). | Newsletter-speak. "I hope this finds you well." Walls of text. |

#### Email (Industry / Booking)

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Lead with the ask. Include the link. One clear CTA. | Lengthy intros. Overselling. Attachments without context. |

#### In-Person / Live

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Talk craft. Reference the history when relevant. Let the work speak first. | Name-drop as an opener. Undersell either. |

## 2. Social Media Strategy

### Platform Priority Ranking

| Rank | Platform | Why |
|------|----------|-----|
| 1 | **Instagram** | Core hub — visual brand home. Promoters and fans both live here. Reels algorithm still strong. Best ROI for event and release content. |
| 2 | **TikTok** | Discovery engine — highest organic reach potential in 2026. Set clips, studio moments, and mashup reveals travel fast. Younger fanbase on-ramp. |
| 3 | **YouTube** | Long-form authority — full set recordings, studio sessions, and documentary-style content build credibility with serious fans and press. SEO value is permanent. |
| 4 | **SoundCloud** | DJ/industry credibility — still the standard link for music industry contacts, bookers, and tastemakers. Essential presence. |
| 5 | **Spotify** | Discovery & passive income — artist profile optimization + playlist pitching for original/remix output. |
| 6 | **X (Twitter)** | Industry conversation — less consumer reach, but real-time relevance for festival lineups, music news, and industry networking. Low effort, keep active. |
| 7 | **Facebook** | Legacy reach — older fan demographic (28–45), local event promotion, paid ads targeting. Lower organic priority. |
| 8 | **Mixcloud** | Community/archive — full mix hosting (no takedowns). Secondary to SoundCloud but valuable for longer sets and mixshow archive. |

### Content Pillars

#### Pillar 1: THE CRAFT 🎹

The musician behind the DJ — studio moments, production process, instrument roots, technical insights.

**Content:** Studio session clips, gear walkthroughs, "why this track works" breakdowns, piano/drum moments, engineering stories, Crooklyn Clan remix process.

**This is the differentiator.** No other DJ in his lane has this story to tell.

#### Pillar 2: THE STAGE ⚡

Live performance energy — what it looks, sounds, and feels like when ANIMAL plays.

**Content:** Full set highlights, crowd reaction footage, venue reveals, touring moments, backstage clips, "this moment at [venue]" nostalgia posts.

#### Pillar 3: THE ARCHIVE 🗂️

25 years of stories, receipts, and moments most DJs can't claim.

**Content:** Throwback photos/videos from Playboy Mansion, WMC, Groove Cruise, major stages. "Then vs. Now" format. Stories from residencies. Context on remix credits. Radio history.

**This builds authority with zero new production cost.**

#### Pillar 4: NEW MUSIC 🎧

Releases, remixes, mashups, collabs — what's coming and what just dropped.

**Content:** Track teasers, release countdowns, behind-the-track stories, "here's what inspired this," playlist additions, performance premieres.

#### Pillar 5: THE SCENE 🌐

ANIMAL as a node in electronic music culture — curating, reacting, participating.

**Content:** Reactions to new releases, festival commentary, industry takes, "what I'm listening to," artist shoutouts, genre discussion.

**Builds cultural authority and invites engagement.**

|---------|--------------|
| Original single/EP/album drop | Music release announcement |

| New residency booked | Residency announcement |

**Frequency:** Minimum 1 press release per quarter. Do not over-release — each PR touch should carry weight.

### EPK Checklist

An EPK that actually gets read:

- [ ] **Artist bio** — short (100 words), medium (300 words), full (600 words) versions
- [ ] **High-resolution photos** — minimum 3: performance, studio, portrait. At least 2 horizontal, 1 vertical. 300dpi minimum.
- [ ] **Logo files** — PNG with transparent background, multiple sizes
- [ ] **Select discography** — top originals, remixes, and Crooklyn Clan credits with streaming links
- [ ] **Career highlights** — bullet-point format: residencies, notable stages, radio first, co-signs
- [ ] **Video links** — 1 full live set (best quality available), 1 studio/process clip, 1 crowd-reaction clip
- [ ] **SoundCloud/Spotify links** — current, active, and professional
- [ ] **Contact information** — booking email, management contact, press contact (separate if possible)
- [ ] **Technical rider** — separate doc, included or linked
- [ ] **Social media handles** — all platforms, consistent handle across platforms
- [ ] **Press quotes** — if you have them; if not, remove the section rather than leaving it empty
- [ ] **Availability window** — when/where you're available to book (general regions/months)

**Format:** Hosted as a Linktree-style page or PDF. Never email giant ZIP files to promoters — link to a hosted EPK.

### Pitch Angles That Actually Work

#### Angle 1: The Origin Story — "The Piano Kid Who Became a Legend"

Classical piano at 6, percussion, vocals — the full musician path. EDM is often criticized for being technically shallow. ANIMAL is the counterargument. Pitch to outlets doing "behind the DJ booth" style features.

#### Angle 2: The Crooklyn Clan Connection — "The Man Behind Your Favorite Mashup"

Most music fans have heard ANIMAL's work and don't know it. Eminem, Snoop, Linkin Park, Missy, Migos — these are cultural touchstones. Pitch the "uncredited genius" narrative to hip-hop and mainstream outlets. This is a crossover story.

#### Angle 3: The Radio First — "How ANIMAL Brought EDM to FM in the Carolinas"

Radio history is legitimacy. Being first at something is a hook. Pitch to music industry trades (Billboard, Variety) and regional outlets with nostalgia for the EDM radio era. Connects to the larger narrative of EDM's mainstream breakthrough.

#### Angle 4: The Training Pedigree — "Trained by the People Who Made the Records You Know"

Danny Wyatt (Curtis Mayfield, Norah Jones), John Siket (Phish, DMB), Mike White (AC/DC, Whitney Houston). This is an extraordinary engineering lineage. Pitch to production/studio-focused outlets (Sound on Sound, Tape Op, Attack Magazine). Appeals to the producer/engineer audience.

#### Angle 5: The Boise Angle — "25 Years in EDM, Now Redefining a Market"

Local press LOVES the "big-city credibility meets our town" angle. Boise has a growing music scene. ANIMAL brings real credentials to a market that rarely sees them. Pitch to local outlets for feature coverage — often leads to regional broadcast interest.

### Interview/Feature Outreach Strategy

1. **Build the media list:** 25–40 outlets across tiers (national dance/EDM, mainstream music, local, podcast)
2. **Personalize pitches:** One paragraph of genuine connection before the ask. Reference a recent article they published. Show you actually read the outlet.
3. **Lead with the angle, not the bio:** Editors get hundreds of bio pitches. Lead with the story idea.
4. **Offer exclusives strategically:** For a new single, offer one publication early access for 48 hours. Creates urgency and relationship capital.
5. **Follow-up once, professionally:** Wait 7–10 business days, send one brief follow-up. No more.
6. **Podcast circuit:** 5–8 podcast appearances per year builds search presence, audio clips, and audience crossover with minimal barrier to entry.

## 5. Booking & Promoter Marketing

### One-Sheet Template Structure

A one-sheet is a single page. One. Page.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [PHOTO — high energy, performance shot]     │
│                                              │
│  ERIC "ANIMAL" MILLS                         │
│  DJ · PRODUCER · MUSICIAN                   │
│                                              │
│  [2-sentence positioning statement]          │
│                                              │
│  NOTABLE STAGES & RESIDENCIES                │
│  • Playboy Mansion (LA)                      │
│  • Winter Music Conference (Miami)           │
│  • Groove Cruise                             │
│  • [3–4 more top credits]                    │
│                                              │
│  SHARED STAGES / REMIXED                     │
│  Jay-Z · Skrillex · Nicki Minaj · [etc.]     │
│                                              │
│  GENRE: Electronic / EDM / Open Format       │
│                                              │
│  [1 crowd photo or performance still]        │
│                                              │
│  BOOKING: [email]  WEB: [url]                │
│  @HANDLE across IG · TikTok · SoundCloud     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Make a separate version for: (1) Club/nightlife, (2) Festival, (3) Corporate/private events

### What Promoters Need to See

In order of what they actually care about:

1. **Draw proof** — Photos and video of packed rooms, crowd energy, real people losing their minds
2. **Credibility anchors** — The two or three big names that make them stop scrolling (Playboy Mansion, WMC, name-drops they recognize)
3. **Genre fit** — Is this right for my crowd? Be specific about what you do — "Electronic/EDM, open-format capable, house-through-hip-hop fluency"
4. **Professional execution** — A clean EPK, responsive communication, clear rider
5. **Social proof** — Active, real-looking social media with real engagement (not follower count — engagement)
6. **Price clarity** — They hate guessing. Have a rate card ready, but keep it off the one-sheet; send with inquiry response.

### Outreach Strategy for New Markets

#### Research First

Identify 5–10 top venues in target market. Look at who they book, what nights they run, what the demographic is.

#### The Warm Path (Preferred)

- Who do you know who's played there? Get an intro.
- Tag venues authentically in event content — they notice.
- Play the market once (smaller show) before pitching headline.

#### The Cold Path

- Find the talent buyer's name (check LinkedIn, call the venue, look at event credits on social).
- Email: 3 sentences max. Photo, link to EPK, specific ask (date range, type of night).
- Subject line: Direct. "DJ Booking Inquiry — Eric ANIMAL Mills // [Date Range]"
- Follow up once in 7–10 days.

#### Regional Cluster Strategy

Don't book one show in a new city — book a 3–5 date cluster in a region. More cost-efficient, builds local relationships faster, justifies travel.

### Festival Submission Strategy and Timeline

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| 12–18 months before festival | Submit to large festivals (Electric Daisy, Ultra, Coachella dance stages) — early cycle |
| 9–12 months before | Mid-tier festival submissions; most booking decisions made here |
| 6–9 months before | Regional and emerging festival circuit |
| 3–6 months before | Late-cycle local and boutique festivals; last-minute fill slots |
| Ongoing | Electronic cruise submissions (Groove Cruise, Holy Ship) — separate cycle, earlier |

**Submission tools:**
- Sonicbids / GigSalad for smaller festivals
- Direct to talent buyers for mid-to-large festivals
- IEBA roster if pursuing agency representation

**What festival submissions need:**
- One-sheet (festival version)
- 30–60 minute recorded live set link (SoundCloud/Mixcloud/YouTube)
- Technical rider
- Brief "why this festival" statement — personalize, don't blast

### Residency Pitch Strategy

Residencies are the highest-value booking in terms of stability, relationship-building, and market penetration.

**Pitch requirements:**
- Demonstrate history (existing or past residencies are the strongest proof)
- Propose the concept — it's not "ANIMAL plays every Friday," it's "ANIMAL brings [X night] to life as [specific concept]"
- Show local or touring fan base that will drive recurring attendance
- Include revenue-sharing or base guarantee proposal based on the venue tier

**Target residency contexts:**
- Boutique hotel bars (Boise market opportunity — underserved by EDM talent)
- Casino entertainment programs (Mountain West/Pacific Northwest circuit)
- Electronic cruises (Groove Cruise is already in history — pursue re-engagement)
- Resort/destination venues (Park City, Sun Valley, Vegas strip — proximity to Boise)

**The pitch deck for residency:** 5–7 slides max: artist positioning, concept for the night, sample setlist or musical direction, social reach/audience demographics, logistics/rider overview, proposed terms.

## 6. Email Marketing

- QR code to sign-up form at merch table or on projected visuals
- "Text [keyword] to [number]" SMS capture (use a tool like Community or EZTexting)

#### Online

- Link in bio → landing page with lead magnet (exclusive mix download, early access, presale)
- "Join the inner circle" framing — not a newsletter, an access tier

#### At Release

- Pre-save campaigns that capture email (SubmitHub, Show.co, ToneDen)
- Collaborator list swap (ethical cross-promotion with artists ANIMAL collaborates with)

**Tools:** Mailchimp (up to 500 free), Klaviyo (best for segmentation), or ConvertKit (creator-focused). Pick one, commit.

### Newsletter Cadence and Content

**Frequency:** 2x per month is the floor. 3–4x during active release or touring periods.

**Format:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|

| **Hook** | One sentence — what's in this email and why it matters |
| **THE DROP** | New music, remix, or release news |
| **THE STAGE** | Upcoming shows, ticket links |
| **FROM THE VAULT** | One archive story or throwback — builds lore |
| **IN ROTATION** | 3 tracks ANIMAL is listening to right now |
| **CTA** | One per email, not five |

**Subject line principle:** Write subject lines like you'd text a friend.

### Segmentation

## 7. Paid Advertising Strategy

### Meta (FB/IG) Ads

**Use cases:** Release promotion, event tickets, fanbase growth in target markets

**Campaign structure:**

| Campaign Type | Purpose |
|---------------|---------|
| **Awareness campaigns** | Reel-based, optimized for video views — top of funnel, new cities |
| **Engagement campaigns** | Push to warm audiences (page followers, video viewers) around releases |
| **Conversion campaigns** | Ticketed events — retarget social engagers and lookalike audiences based on ticket buyer email list |

**Targeting strategy:**

| Target Type | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Interest targeting** | DJ culture, specific festivals (Ultra, EDC), specific artists ANIMAL shares audiences with |
| **Geographic** | Event-specific (5–30 mile radius for shows); broader for streaming releases |
| **Lookalike audiences** | Upload fan email list → 1–3% lookalike in target cities |
| **Retargeting** | 30/60-day video viewers, profile visitors, website visitors |

**Creative:** Short (6–15 sec) video ads outperform static for music. Use best-performing organic content as ad creative — don't produce separate ad assets until you have budget to scale.

**Minimum viable budget:** $10–20/day for release pushes, $25–50/day for event promotion in event window. Don't run campaigns below $5/day — insufficient data to optimize.

### TikTok Ads

**Use cases:** Release discovery, touring market awareness, younger audience funnel

**Best formats:**

| Format | Use |
|--------|-----|
| **TopView** | Expensive, brand-level |
| **In-Feed Video** | Primary tool |
| **Spark Ads** | Boost existing organic content — most cost-efficient option |

**Strategy:** Use Spark Ads to boost high-performing organic TikToks during release windows. Far cheaper than creating dedicated ad creative and leverages existing social proof.

**Targeting:** Music interest targeting + Pixel-based retargeting once TikTok Pixel is installed on website.

### Spotify Marquee / Discovery Mode

| Feature | Use |
|---------|-----|
| **Marquee** | Paid push notification to followers when a new release drops. Best used for EP or album releases, not every single. Estimated $0.55–0.65 per click-through. Budget $200–500 per major release. |
| **Discovery Mode** | Opt tracks into Spotify's algorithm boost in exchange for lower royalty rate on those streams. Good for catalog tracks you want to resurface. Use selectively on tracks with high conversion potential. |

**Playlist pitching (free):** Submit unreleased tracks to Spotify editorial via Spotify for Artists at least 7 days before release. This is non-negotiable — do it for every release.

## 8. Partnership & Collaboration Strategy

### Brand Partnership Target Categories

#### Tier 1 — Natural fit, easiest pitch

- DJ/audio equipment (Pioneer DJ, Rane, Native Instruments, Serato, Sennheiser, Shure)
- EDM/music festivals as official artist partner
- Recording software (Ableton, FL Studio, Native Instruments)

#### Tier 2 — Lifestyle alignment

- Premium spirits (rum, vodka, whiskey brands with nightlife presence)
- Energy drinks with electronic music positioning (Red Bull, Monster, GHOST)
- Headphone/audio consumer brands (Sony, Beats, Bose — longer shot without larger numbers)
- Streetwear/fashion brands targeting nightlife demographic

#### Tier 3 — Emerging opportunity

- NFT/Web3 music platforms (selective — be careful with credibility)
- Cannabis/CBD brands in legal states (growing nightlife sponsorship category)
- Travel and hospitality (airline partnerships for touring artists; boutique hotel brands)

**Pitch requirements for brand partnerships:**

- Media kit with audience demographics (age, gender, geography, engagement rate)
- Proposed activation concept (what would ANIMAL actually do with this brand?)
- Rate card for posts, appearances, and exclusivity
- Content examples showing brand-adjacent posts done naturally

### Artist Collaboration Outreach Strategy

Collaboration is one of the fastest audience-expansion tools available.

**Target collaboration tiers:**

| Tier | Strategy |
|------|----------|
| **Peers** | Artists in the same tier with complementary audiences (cross-pollinate) |
| **Emerging** | Artists with upward trajectory — their growing audience becomes ANIMAL's awareness |
| **Legacy artists** | Reconnect and formalize the Crooklyn Clan-era relationships. A "legacy remix" with an artist from that catalog era could generate serious press. |

**Outreach approach:**

1. Start with genuine engagement on their content (not parasocial — actual comments)
2. Identify a specific creative angle ("I've been building something that would hit different with your vocal on it")
3. Reach out via DM or through mutual contact with a clear, specific ask
4. No generic "let's collab" messages — bring the idea

**Remix trade strategy:** Offer an official remix of an up-and-coming artist's track in exchange for a return remix. Both parties gain cross-audience exposure. Low cost, high relationship value.

### Festival/Event Sponsor Partnership Angles

When pitching to events as a sponsored artist (vs. just a paid performer):

- **Content production value:** ANIMAL can generate high-quality content for the festival
- **Workshop/masterclass offer:** 25 years of knowledge = credibility for educational programming
- **Brand ambassador for festival sponsors:** If a sponsor activates at the festival, ANIMAL can be part of that activation
- **Radio connection:** Curtis Media / FM history is a credible angle for media-sponsor integration at festivals

### Sync Licensing / Music Placement Strategy

Sync is one of the highest-ROI long-tail revenue channels for producers.

**Getting in position:**

1. Audit the catalog — identify tracks with strong sync potential (high energy for trailers/sports; emotive/atmospheric for TV drama; banger format for ads/gaming)
2. Register everything with ASCAP or BMI (if not already done) and ensure metadata is clean in distribution
3. Create a "sync catalog" page on website: mood-tagged tracks, BPM, key, clear licensing CTA

**Outreach channels:**

| Channel | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Music libraries** | Musicbed, Artlist, Epidemic Sound (for non-exclusive licensing) |
| **Sync agencies** | Jingle Punks, Position Music, Heavy Hitters (for exclusive/co-rep deals) |
| **Direct to music supervisors** | Guild of Music Supervisors directory; LinkedIn research |
| **Music placement services** | Taxi, SubmitHub (film/sync section) |

**Pitch format for supervisors:** Short email. 3 sentences. "I have [X tracks] in [mood/genre]. Here's a Soundcloud link to the sync-ready catalog. All cleared, no samples, can deliver stems within 24 hours." That's it. They don't want a bio.

## 10. Quick Wins This Week

Ten things to do now. Not eventually — this week.

1. **Audit every social profile for consistency.** Same name, same bio language, same link. If your IG bio says something different from your Facebook bio, fix it today. Promoters cross-reference everything.

2. **Set up a proper link-in-bio page.** Linktree, Koji, or a simple landing page. It should have: streaming links, booking contact, EPK link, email sign-up, upcoming shows. One URL that does all the work.

3. **Pull and document your 5 best crowd/performance videos.** Not the ones you think are good — the ones where the crowd energy is undeniable. These are your most important assets. Get them optimized and ready to deploy.

4. **Write (or update) your three bio lengths:** 100 words, 300 words, 600 words. If you have to write it fresh every time someone asks, you're losing momentum. Lock these in. Have them in a notes doc, ready to paste.

5. **Set up a dedicated booking email address.** booking@yourdomain.com (or similar). Forward it to wherever you check email. The professionalism signal is worth the 10 minutes it takes.

6. **Register on Spotify for Artists and verify your profile.** If you're not controlling your Spotify artist profile, someone else's metadata is controlling it. Verify, add a photo, add a bio, pin your best tracks.

7. **Email 5 promoters or talent buyers you already have a relationship with.** Not to pitch — just to reconnect. "Hey, I've got some new material and I'm scheduling out Q3. What's your calendar looking like?" Warm outreach is 10x more effective than cold.

8. **Post the best archive content you have.** One throwback post — a photo, a clip, a story from a memorable gig. No production required. This week. Post it. See what it does.

9. **Identify your best sync-ready tracks and add a "licensing" section to your website.** Even a basic page: "For sync licensing inquiries, contact [email]." Add mood tags and streaming links. This is passive revenue infrastructure you can build in an afternoon.

10. **Claim your Google Knowledge Panel and verify your artist profiles.** Search your name. If there's a Knowledge Panel, claim it at Google's support page. Verify your artist profile on Apple Music, Amazon Music, Deezer, Tidal. These are free and take 15 minutes each. They make you look like you've been at this for 25 years — because you have.
